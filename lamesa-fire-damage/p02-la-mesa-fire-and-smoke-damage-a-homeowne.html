What restoration actually involves

Good fire restoration is a set order of work, not one quick clean.

First is the emergency step. Crews board up broken windows and openings, tarp the roof, and pump out standing water so the home does not take on more damage overnight.

Next is water removal and drying, because the water used to fight the fire is often the bigger problem. Wet drywall and framing grow mold within a day or two if they are not dried out.

Then comes soot and residue cleanup. The type of smoke matters here. Dry smoke from a fast, hot fire cleans up differently than the greasy, sticky residue from a slow, smoky fire. A trained crew tests the residue and matches the cleaning method to it.

Odor removal is its own step. Smoke smell hides in insulation, ductwork, and framing. Real odor work uses tools like thermal fogging, ozone, or hydroxyl treatment, not just air fresheners.

Last is repair and rebuild, from new drywall and paint to full room reconstruction, so the home looks like it did before the fire.

Content and belongings

Furniture, clothes, and keepsakes can often be saved even when they look ruined. Many crews run a pack-out, where they take your things to a facility, clean and deodorize them, and store them until the home is ready. Photos, documents, and electronics need special handling and should not be cleaned with normal household products.

Choosing a pro in La Mesa

Look for a crew that answers the phone any hour, since fire damage does not wait for business hours. Ask if their technicians are IICRC certified, which is the main standard for restoration work.

Ask whether they handle the full job, from board-up to the final rebuild, so you are not managing three different companies. A crew that also works directly with insurance takes a lot of stress off you.

Ask for a clear written scope before work starts. You want to know what is being cleaned, what is being replaced, and roughly how long it will take.

What it costs and how long it takes

Cost depends on how much of the home the fire and smoke reached, how much water went in, and how much rebuild is needed. A single smoke-damaged room is a small job. A fire that spread through the attic and several rooms is a large one.

Timelines run the same way. Cleanup and drying can take a few days. A full rebuild can take weeks. A good crew gives you a realistic timeline up front instead of a guess.

What to do first

Stay out until the fire department says the home is safe. Structures can be weak, and air inside can still be toxic after a fire.

Call your insurance company and start a claim. Take photos and video of every room before anyone touches anything. Keep receipts for a hotel, food, and anything you buy while you are out.

Do not wipe soot off walls yourself. Wiping the wrong way grinds soot in and turns a cleanable surface into one that has to be repainted or replaced. Do not turn the HVAC back on, since it can spread smoke and soot through the whole house.

Get a professional fire damage crew out fast. Soot is acidic, and every day it sits it does more permanent damage to metal, stone, and finishes.
